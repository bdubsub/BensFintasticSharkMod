# BFS:AO The Trench: Minecraft 26.2 ModJam Plan

## Status

This document is the design, audit, animation, and migration plan for a future NeoForge 26.2 project. It does not describe implemented Minecraft 26.2 behavior.

EnVy confirmed ownership of the Ben's Fintastic Sharks code and authored assets on August 9, 2026. The new project can reuse them. Repository and publishing metadata still need to move to EnVy or `MCEnvision` so public ownership matches current authority.

The active Forge 1.20.1 BFS phase remains separate. The ModJam entry must be a new repository and CurseForge project rather than an in place update of the current mod.

## Current creative direction

The approved working title is **BFS:AO The Trench**.

The story no longer uses a magical time rift or a fracture between dimensions. The Kraken physically drags the player to the bottom of a Mariana inspired trench and through a dense cold barrier into a sealed second ocean beneath the known seabed. The hidden ocean has no reachable surface. It contains a prehistoric ecosystem, legendary ocean beings, wreckage, and everything required for underwater survival.

The title directly names the immense trench and hidden world beneath it. There is no magical rift or portal in the story.

The player is rescued by neutral Mermaids, learns to survive entirely underwater, explores the hidden ecosystem, builds the equipment required to survive extreme pressure, and eventually escapes upward into the vanilla world while the Kraken tries to prevent the ascent.

## Executive recommendation

Build a new NeoForge 26.2 mod with a custom hidden ocean world presented as a physically connected layer beneath the vanilla ocean. A separate dimension is acceptable as an implementation container for terrain scale, generation, lighting, and performance, but the game must never describe it as another universe or a teleport destination. Descent and ascent transitions should look continuous.

Reuse the strongest BFS systems and authored assets:

* Aquatic movement and body pitch concepts.
* Predator hunger, prey selection, investigation, pursuit, bite timing, grab, latch, and release contracts.
* Disturbance and blood detection.
* Creature scale, variants, beaching, fleeing, schooling, and species behaviors.
* Existing geometry, textures, animations, items, tags, loot patterns, and data generation where they serve the new ecosystem.

Rebuild loader integration, rendering, synchronized state, persistence, networking, spawning, world generation, commands, and story progression against NeoForge 26.2 and GeckoLib 5. Do not mechanically copy the Forge 1.20.1 source tree.

The required new creature roster is now five models:

* Mosasaurus.
* Kraken.
* Mermaid.
* Otodus Megalodon.
* Purussaurus.

Existing BFS creatures provide the remaining predators, prey, ambient life, and visual variety. A new Whale is no longer required for the ModJam release.

## Contest constraints

The official ModJam theme is **Echoes of the Past**. The contest requires a new project for Minecraft 26.1 or newer, and submissions close September 1, 2026 at 5:00 PM GMT plus 3. Judging weights originality, fun, and visuals at thirty percent each, with downloads at ten percent.

The hidden prehistoric ocean directly supports the theme. Extinct species are literal echoes of the past, Mermaids and the Kraken embody maritime legends, and wrecks preserve human history trapped beneath the barrier.

The public project must feel like a new underwater survival experience, not a BFS version update. BFS supplies the ecosystem foundation. The hidden trench, survival loop, Mermaids, escape vehicle, pressure progression, new structures, and Kraken pursuit establish the new identity.

The contest rules prohibit generated project avatars and gallery images. Use authored screenshots, Blockbench renders, or hand made art for the project page.

## Inspiration boundary

The supplied story reference explains the hidden ecosystem premise from *The Meg*: a dense cold thermocline is mistaken for the bottom, but a warmer isolated ocean exists underneath it and contains prehistoric life.

Use that broad premise and underwater survival tone only. Do not copy characters, names, facilities, dialogue, rescue scenes, beach attacks, poison plans, or the film's sequence of events. BFS:AO needs its own story structure:

* The player is the victim rather than a rescue specialist.
* The Kraken causes the descent.
* Mermaids perform the rescue.
* There is no reachable surface in the hidden ocean.
* Survival, crafting, exploration, pressure management, and ascent are the core loop.
* The Megalodon is part of the ecosystem, not the sole escaped movie monster.
* The player escapes the hidden ocean instead of defending a modern beach.

## Story canon

### The hidden world

The known ocean appears to end at the bottom of an immense trench. Sonar, light, and ordinary travel cannot reveal what lies below because a dense layer of freezing, mineral rich water forms a natural barrier.

Beneath that layer is a second ocean. Geothermal vents, bioluminescent organisms, and unfamiliar chemistry keep it warmer and alive. It has remained isolated for millions of years. Prehistoric animals, modern species pulled through weak points, and creatures from old maritime legends coexist there.

The hidden ocean is physically below the vanilla world in the story. It has a stone and thermocline ceiling instead of a sky. There is no open air surface to swim toward.

### The Kraken

The Kraken is an intelligent territorial animal that understands currents and the cold barrier. It hunts near weak points in the trench, grabs vessels and swimmers, and drags them into its world.

It deliberately takes the player. Its motive does not need spoken exposition. Possible interpretations include hunting, curiosity, territorial collection, or protecting the hidden ecosystem from surface intrusion.

The Kraken is the main force preventing escape, but it should not behave like an evil human villain. The final encounter is about surviving and breaking through the barrier, not necessarily killing it.

### The Mermaids

Mermaids are neutral inhabitants adapted to the hidden ocean. They understand the Kraken, pressure zones, safe currents, and ruins. A group finds the player after the descent and prevents immediate death.

They are not automatically obedient allies. Their trust grows through nonviolent behavior, aid, offerings, rescue, and respect for the ecosystem. The story must remain completable if the player damages that relationship, but the safer and clearer route comes through cooperation.

### The player goal

The player's first goal is oxygen and safety. The long term goal is to build equipment capable of surviving the cold barrier and extreme ascent, reach the trench passage, escape the Kraken, and return to the vanilla ocean.

Everything required must exist underwater. The player cannot rely on reaching a surface, importing a complete technology tree from the Overworld, or dying to skip progression.

## Player journey

### Act 1. Taken below

1. The player encounters abnormal currents, damaged wreckage, or a deep sea disturbance in a vanilla ocean.
2. Warning bubbles, pressure sounds, fleeing fish, and distant tentacles make the danger readable.
3. The Kraken attacks and physically grabs the player.
4. A real time cutscene follows the player being dragged down the trench past wreckage, fleeing animals, pressure zones, and distant silhouettes.
5. The destination chunks finish preparing before the cutscene reaches the cold barrier.
6. The barrier becomes fully opaque through cold haze, darkness, bubbles, and Kraken ink. The server transfers the player during this covered frame.
7. A custom cutscene overlay remains visible across the world change so vanilla's `Loading terrain` presentation never appears.
8. The scene resumes immediately below the barrier and flows into the Mermaid rescue without returning control between sequences.

### Act 2. Mermaid rescue

1. Mermaids find the player before the first oxygen window becomes unfair.
2. They repel or distract the nearest Kraken tentacle and carry the player to a protected sanctuary.
3. The sanctuary provides a breathable bubble, temporary water breathing, or an equivalent safe recovery method.
4. Visual signs, short subtitles, and gestures establish that there is no surface above.
5. The player receives or learns the first survival recipe rather than receiving the entire progression path.

### Seamless descent cutscene

The descent is a real time in game sequence, not a prerecorded video. It makes the hidden ocean feel physically beneath the vanilla world while concealing the technical world transfer.

#### Sequence

1. A Kraken tentacle enters the player's view and begins a server authoritative capture.
2. The camera widens or moves to a controlled third person angle as the Kraken pulls the player downward.
3. The descent passes authored trench silhouettes, wreck fragments, rock walls, bubbles, and fleeing BFS creatures.
4. Pressure and cold intensify while subtitles and visual cues preserve accessibility.
5. The cold barrier and Kraken ink cover the complete frame.
6. The server transfers the player to a validated prepared destination while the frame is opaque.
7. The custom overlay survives the client level change and continues rendering instead of showing `Loading terrain`.
8. Visibility returns beneath the barrier, the Kraken loosens its hold, and Mermaids enter the same sequence.
9. The rescue ends only after the player is placed safely inside the initial sanctuary and every forced camera, passenger, invulnerability, and input state is cleared.

#### Technical contract

* Prepare and validate destination chunks before starting the irreversible transfer phase.
* Use temporary bounded chunk tickets and release them after placement or timeout.
* Keep the server authoritative over capture, invulnerability, transfer, placement, and completion.
* Synchronize only cutscene phase, camera target, timing, accessibility intensity, and presentation markers to the client.
* Stage the apparent long descent with controlled camera paths, animated entities, particles, and trench set pieces. Do not physically move the authoritative player thousands of blocks through ordinary terrain.
* Hold the authoritative player in a collision safe capture state until the covered transfer commits.
* Replace or cover the vanilla receiving level screen through supported NeoForge client hooks when possible.
* Use a narrowly scoped mixin only if 26.2 exposes no supported way to preserve the overlay across the level change, and document the exact target and failure fallback.
* Transfer during a fully opaque frame so short scheduling differences cannot reveal unloaded terrain.
* Keep the cutscene bounded. A stalled phase must release the player or restore the saved vanilla position.
* If destination preparation fails, cancel the capture and return control without moving the player.
* If the player disconnects, resume at the initial sanctuary or saved vanilla position according to the last committed phase.
* Protect the player from ordinary damage during forced movement without making that protection persist afterward.
* Preserve inventory, effects, allowed passengers, and multiplayer participation state.

#### Player controls and accessibility

* The first story descent may be mandatory, but provide an emergency skip that jumps to validated sanctuary placement.
* Repeat descents should be shorter or skippable.
* Camera motion, shake, blur, tint, and distortion use client intensity settings.
* Do not use rapid flashing.
* Provide subtitles for pressure, Kraken, current, and Mermaid cues.
* Support a first person comfort mode if the cinematic camera causes motion sickness.

#### Acceptance

* `Loading terrain` never becomes visible during a successful descent.
* The cutscene does not expose empty chunks, sky, void, portal effects, or a frozen frame.
* Audio, animation, particles, camera, and world transfer remain synchronized within acceptable client latency.
* The sequence recovers safely from skip, death, disconnect, destination timeout, and multiplayer desynchronization.
* The final frame flows directly into Mermaid rescue and sanctuary placement.

### Act 3. Survive the second ocean

1. The player gathers fibers, shells, salvage, teeth, chitin, minerals, and thermal crystals.
2. Early tools extend oxygen and allow safe gathering near the sanctuary.
3. Existing BFS animals establish the food chain and region hazards.
4. Mosasaurus controls open prehistoric water.
5. Purussaurus controls root systems, flooded caves, and shallow ruins beneath the ceiling shelf.
6. Otodus Megalodon patrols the largest basin and responds to heavy disturbance and blood.
7. The player discovers wreck logs and Mermaid pictographs explaining earlier failed ascents.

### Act 4. Build for ascent

1. The player builds a reliable oxygen system.
2. Pressure resistant materials unlock deeper salvage and thermal vents.
3. Three escape subsystems replace the former anchor concept:
   * Pressure Hull.
   * Thermal Lift Engine.
   * Mermaid Resonance Navigator.
4. These systems combine into an Abyssal Ascender, bathysphere, or compact submersible.
5. The vehicle name and final appearance remain art decisions, but its function must be clear.

### Act 5. Escape the trench

1. The player brings the completed ascent system to the passage beneath the cold barrier.
2. The Kraken detects the attempt and creates current pulses, tentacle strikes, and obstruction hazards.
3. Damage or defensive actions repel individual tentacles and open travel windows. The objective is ascent, not reducing one health bar to zero.
4. Mermaid assistance depends on reputation but is not required for basic completion.
5. The player crosses the barrier and returns to a safe vanilla ocean location.
6. Multiplayer companions in the same escape attempt return together and retain progression credit.
7. A completed player can later build a stable descent vehicle to revisit the hidden world.

## Underwater survival design

### Core loop

The target feeling is exploration and survival similar to an underwater survival game while remaining recognizably Minecraft:

1. Leave a safe bubble with a limited oxygen window.
2. Gather resources and salvage.
3. Return, craft, and improve equipment.
4. Reach a new depth or region.
5. Learn creature behavior rather than treating every animal as a combat encounter.
6. Recover an escape component or technology clue.
7. Expand safe travel until ascent becomes possible.

### Oxygen progression

The first release should use a small, readable progression:

* Temporary Mermaid Breath prevents an unfair opening death.
* Kelp Air Bladder provides a short emergency refill or ascent impulse.
* Shell Rebreather slows air consumption.
* Compressed Air Tank adds a visible reserve.
* Mermaid Sanctuary bubbles and thermal vent pockets provide fixed safe zones.
* The ascent vehicle supplies its own oxygen during the final sequence.

Do not hide required oxygen information. Show remaining reserve with an accessible HUD element, sound cue, particles, and subtitles.

### Pressure progression

Pressure must create region gating without becoming constant unavoidable damage:

* Safe shelf regions require no special equipment.
* Deep basin regions warn the player before applying effects.
* Reinforced diving equipment extends the safe depth.
* The pressure hull is required for the cold barrier and final ascent.
* Creative and spectator players ignore pressure.

Pressure checks must use region or depth bands at controlled intervals, not expensive continuous block scans.

### Resources

All required resources exist below the barrier:

* Kelp and plant fibers for early equipment.
* Shell, bone, teeth, and chitin for tools and reinforcement.
* Wreck scrap and glass for tanks and vehicle parts.
* Thermal crystals and vent minerals for power.
* Mermaid crafted components for navigation and pressure control.
* Food from plants, fish, and ordinary Minecraft mechanics.

Avoid making endangered or neutral story creatures mandatory crafting ingredients.

### Safe spaces

The hidden ocean has no surface, but it can contain breathable spaces:

* Mermaid bubble sanctuaries.
* Sealed wreck compartments.
* Geothermal air pockets.
* Player built pressure pods as a stretch feature.

At least one safe space must be reachable from every required progression region without perfect equipment.

### Death and recovery

Death must not bypass or destroy the story:

* Save the last validated sanctuary or pressure pod.
* Respawn an active trench player at that safe location when technically safe.
* Preserve vanilla death inventory rules unless the project explicitly adds a recovery system.
* Never respawn a player inside the cold barrier, a Kraken grab, solid terrain, or an unloaded structure.
* Provide an operator recovery command and automatic fallback to the initial sanctuary.

## Hidden ocean world design

### Technical representation

Use a custom world or dimension as the implementation container. Present it as a contiguous physical space under the trench.

The cutscene backed transition should:

* Save the player's safe vanilla return position.
* Prepare the destination before the descent reaches its covered transfer frame.
* Apply the real time Kraken descent and cold barrier visuals.
* Transfer the player only while the server owns the transition state.
* Place the player at a validated hidden ocean entry volume.
* Keep the custom cutscene overlay above the vanilla receiving level screen.
* Hide ordinary portal particles, portal language, and `Loading terrain`.
* Handle mounts, passengers, disconnects, death, skips, and multiplayer groups.
* Release temporary destination chunk tickets after completion or failure.

### Ceiling and lighting

The world has no sky or surface. Its upper boundary is rock, ice, dense water, or a rendered thermocline ceiling. Light comes from geothermal areas, organisms, ruins, and diffused chemical glow.

Brightness must remain playable without removing the abyssal mood. Required paths use landmarks, color, particles, Mermaid songs, and readable silhouettes.

### Region 1. Sanctuary Shelf

The safest modern and mythic overlap.

Content:

* Mermaid sanctuary and tutorial bubble.
* Small wrecks and basic salvage.
* Atlantic Cod, Atlantic Salmon, dolphins, turtles, stingrays, octopuses, jellyfish, and smaller sharks.
* Early oxygen materials.
* First escape subsystem clues.

### Region 2. Fossil Forest

A dense prehistoric shelf of roots, giant plants, bones, and flooded caverns.

Content:

* Purussaurus ambush territories.
* Mosasaurus routes through deeper channels.
* Chitin, bone, resin, and reinforced material progression.
* Prehistoric ruins and failed expedition remains.
* Pressure Hull resources.

### Region 3. Open Basin

The largest hidden ocean chamber.

Content:

* Otodus Megalodon patrol routes.
* Large schools, Orcas, dolphins, sharks, and prey interactions.
* Major wreck field and vehicle salvage.
* Thermal Lift Engine components.
* Long sight lines and silhouettes that communicate scale.

### Region 4. Hadal Gate

The ascent passage controlled by the Kraken.

Content:

* Cold barrier ceiling.
* Kraken tentacle zones.
* Violent vertical currents and thermal vents.
* Mermaid navigation markers.
* Final assembly site and ascent route.

### Current simulation

Use bounded current fields with center, radius, direction, vertical force, intensity curve, and active state. A service samples affected entities at a controlled interval and applies capped acceleration.

Required rules:

* Ignore spectators.
* Provide a creative bypass.
* Cap velocity and accumulated force.
* Do not pull entities through solid collision.
* Do not force load chunks.
* Allow strong swimmers and the ascent vehicle to compensate.
* Use current fields for the Kraken descent and final escape without simulating every water block.

## Animation production plan

Animation production is required implementation work. The five models are not considered content complete until their rigs, loops, one shot actions, event timing, exports, runtime controllers, and in game presentation pass acceptance.

### Rig audit for every model

Before adding keyframes:

1. Open the untouched source in the current Blockbench GeckoLib plugin.
2. Record model format, texture references, texture size, entity scale, and root bone.
3. Validate unique bone names and parent hierarchy.
4. Inspect pivots, rotations, cube origins, mirrored geometry, and bone axes.
5. Identify bones that must remain gameplay attachment points, such as mouth, head, body, hand, tail, and tentacle tips.
6. Repair invalid pivots or hierarchy only in a working copy.
7. Do not rename authored bones after controllers or render layers reference them.
8. Export a neutral pose screenshot and bone manifest.

### Animation principles

* AI owns world translation. Animations must not fake persistent root movement.
* Use smooth easing and continuous loop tangents rather than linear robotic keyframes.
* Use delayed phase motion through spine, tail, fins, limbs, hair, and tentacles.
* Keep large animals slower and heavier than ordinary sharks.
* Tie loop speed to measured movement speed within clamped bounds.
* Separate ordinary swim, fast pursuit, attack, hurt, death, and scripted actions.
* Use server triggered one shot animations for bites, grabs, strikes, rescue, and escape events.
* Define exact contact frames so gameplay damage and attachment timing match the visible motion.
* Keep visual roll and pitch additive so they do not fight the movement controller.
* Test loops at low, ordinary, and maximum movement speeds.
* Preserve silhouettes and avoid excessive whole body flex that makes large creatures look weightless.

### Mosasaurus clip set

Required clips:

* `idle_swim`, slow body and tail movement.
* `swim`, sustained efficient travel.
* `swim_fast`, powerful pursuit stroke.
* `turn_left` and `turn_right`, additive banking if the runtime supports them cleanly.
* `investigate`, slow head and body alignment.
* `lunge`, committed forward attack preparation.
* `bite`, clear mouth contact and recovery frame.
* `grab_thrashing`, only if its gameplay can hold large prey.
* `hurt`, brief readable response without resetting navigation.
* `death`, loss of control and sinking motion.

Acceptance:

* Tail motion travels from torso to tip without snapping.
* The head remains stable during ordinary travel.
* Fast swim looks heavier than a shark sprint.
* Bite contact aligns with mouth reach and server damage.
* Transitioning from pursuit to recovery does not pop to the neutral pose.

* `descend_capture`, the opening grab and downward pull.

## Aquatic behavior architecture

### Shared state machine

Recommended states:

* `IDLE`.
* `WANDER`.
* `INVESTIGATE`.
* `HUNT`.
* `FLEE`.
* `WARN`.
* `AMBUSH`.
* `GRAB`.
* `RECOVER`.
* `HIDE`.
* `BEACHED` where the region permits it.
* `SCRIPTED_ENCOUNTER`.

Each state owns entry, tick, exit, timeout, navigation, animation intent, and interruption rules. SmartBrainLib can supply sensors and tasks, but there must be one authoritative state owner.

### Swimming controller

Implement a current aware three dimensional controller with:

* Bounded yaw and pitch rates.
* Vertical target dead zone.
* Turn aware thrust reduction.
* Arrival distance separate from attack reach.
* Speed taper from distance and yaw error.
* Surface ceiling and seabed avoidance.
* Current compensation by creature strength.
* Debug telemetry.

### Disturbances

Use bounded immutable records for:

* `LIGHT`, ordinary swimming and small actions.
* `HEAVY`, attacks, vehicle impacts, mining, and collapsing wrecks.
* `BLOOD`, injured creatures.
* `PRESSURE`, barrier pulses, vent eruptions, and Kraken contractions.
* `SONG`, Mermaid guidance, warning, and coordination.

Index records by world and region, expire them centrally, and avoid broad per tick scans.

### Ecological rules

* Diets use tags.
* Predators consider hunger, prey size, distance, local risk, and territory.
* Apex predators displace smaller predators without constant combat.
* Ordinary animals have bounded pursuit and readable warning behavior.
* Story events temporarily constrain normal ecology, then restore it.
* The Megalodon and Mosasaurus must feel distinct from current sharks through mass, turning, territory, and attack commitment.

## New creature behavior matrix

| Creature | Region | Relationship | Core behavior | Story role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mosasaurus | Fossil Forest channels and Open Basin edge | Territorial neutral | Depth patrol, silhouette pass, committed burst pursuit, shelter disengage | Establishes prehistoric scale and guards material routes |
| Purussaurus | Fossil Forest roots and floor | Territorial neutral | Concealment, bottom travel, short ambush, return to territory | Creates learned close range danger |
| Otodus Megalodon | Open Basin | Hunger and disturbance driven | Long patrol, large prey targeting, warning pass, attack run, slow turn | Apex ecosystem threat and major survival event |
| Mermaid | Sanctuary Shelf and ruins | Reputation based neutral | Rescue, guide, sing, interact, defend, retreat | Prevents opening death and supports escape navigation |
| Kraken | Hadal Gate and transition zones | Territorial captor | Descent grab, tentacle zones, pressure pulses, ascent obstruction | Causes the story and controls the final escape |

### Mosasaurus

Reuse large predator pursuit and delayed bite concepts. Give it slower turns, stronger forward commitment, large territory, and shelter based disengagement. It must not be a shark with a different model.

### Purussaurus

Use concealment nodes, bottom movement, muddy visibility, and short bursts. Its danger comes from proximity and terrain knowledge, not indefinite pursuit through open water.

### Otodus Megalodon

Reuse disturbance response, large prey selection, and bounded grab cleanup. Give it deliberate warning passes, a minimum turning radius, and attack runs that create escape windows. It remains part of the ecosystem outside story events.

### Mermaid

Use reputation tiers such as defensive, wary, neutral, trusted, and allied. Trusted Mermaids may reveal landmarks, improve oxygen access, teach recipes, exchange components, guide the ascent route, or interrupt one Kraken action.

Attacking a Mermaid lowers trust and makes nearby Mermaids defensive. Completion remains possible through a slower independent navigation path.

### Kraken

Use a central controller or body with bounded tentacle entities or attack volumes. Avoid one permanently active enormous pathfinding entity.

The Kraken must:

* Capture the player in the intro with a strict timeout and recovery path.
* Remain visible as an ecosystem threat without constantly attacking.
* Use server authoritative grabs and strikes.
* Release every target on interruption.
* Block ascent through objectives and timing rather than a health bar only battle.
* Retreat after escape unless a later story explicitly supports killing it.

## Definition of success

The release succeeds when a player is physically dragged below the known ocean by the Kraken, crosses a cold barrier into a believable second ocean with no surface, survives long enough for Mermaids to rescue them, learns to gather and craft entirely underwater, explores a prehistoric ecosystem populated by BFS creatures, Mosasaurus, Purussaurus, and Otodus Megalodon, builds pressure and ascent technology, escapes the Kraken through the trench, and returns safely to the vanilla world.

It must also start on a clean NeoForge 26.2 client and dedicated server, keep multiplayer progression and entity cleanup correct, maintain bounded current and population costs, render every required creature and animation correctly under OpenGL and Vulkan, and present itself as an original authored ModJam project.
